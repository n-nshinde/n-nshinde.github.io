Beyond the Canopy: How Satellite Data Detection Thresholds Influence Policy Evaluation and Deforestation Behavior

(with Kathryn Baragwanath) 2025. Journal of Environmental Economics and Management, 134: 103219.

PDF (open access) · DOI

Brazil’s official satellite system does not record forest clearings smaller than 6.25 hectares. With more detailed satellite data, we find that a 2008 policy that blacklisted high-deforestation towns cut forest loss by 31%, not the 48% that official data show. Landholders also switched to smaller clearings that the official system cannot see.

Unintended Benefits of Large Irrigation Projects in India

(with Prashikdivya Gajbhiye and Matt Woerman) Revise and resubmit at Journal of Development Economics.

Working paper draft

We study the world’s largest irrigation canal in India and compare places on each side of it. Canal water raised groundwater levels by 80%, which reversed the decline of the water table. Farmers also saved $13 million on irrigation works in their fields, irrigated 20% more land and planted 12% more cash crops.

Gross External Damages of Water Pollution in the United States

(with David A. Keiser, Daniel J. Phaneuf, Catherine L. Kling, Yongjie Ji, Tihitina Andarge, Mohammad Mainul Hoque, and Yau-Huo Shr) NBER Working Paper. Under review.

Working paper draft

This paper gives the first national estimate of the harm caused by nutrient pollution in U.S. rivers and lakes. We follow pollution through 2.5 million river and lake sections. The total harm is $35–100 billion per year. Farms release two-thirds of the pollution, but city wastewater causes more of the harm.

Clearing to Claim: The Perverse Incentives of Land Regularization in the Brazilian Amazon

(with Kathryn Baragwanath) Ronald Coase Institute Working Paper No. 14, 2026. Under review.

Working paper draft · RCI

In the Brazilian Amazon, we compare two government programs that give land titles. We use 1.09 million properties and 20 years of satellite data. When title depends on showing the land is “in use,” forest clearing rises in the four years before the title. When title depends on living on the land before a past date, it does not. Landholders also redraw their property lines to stay just under a size limit in the forest law.
