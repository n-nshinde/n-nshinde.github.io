Research overview

I study how property rights, institutions and environmental policy shape land and water use. My work covers land tenure and deforestation in the Brazilian Amazon, groundwater and irrigation in India, and the social cost of water pollution in the United States, using causal inference and satellite data.

Highlights from recent work

all papers →

  • Clearing land to claim it

    When Brazil grants title for "productive use," deforestation rises in the four years before titling and peaks around the title year. Landholders also redraw boundaries to fall just below Forest Code size thresholds. Ronald Coase Institute WP 14, 2026

  • Environmental registries can reduce rural violence

    Early rollout of Brazil's rural environmental registry (CAR) in Pará cut land conflict events by 11.5% and land-related death threats by 10%. JEEM, 2027

  • What satellites miss

    Brazil's official monitoring misses clearings under 6.25 ha, so it overstates the effect of municipal blacklisting (47.6% vs. 31.2%), and landholders shifted toward smaller clearings. JEEM, 2025

  • The cost of water pollution in the U.S.

    First national estimates of the damages from nutrient pollution: $35–100 billion per year, with municipal wastewater contributing more than agriculture. NBER Working Paper

  • Canals can refill aquifers

    Surface water from the world's largest irrigation canal raised groundwater levels by 80% of baseline in India. R&R, Journal of Development Economics
